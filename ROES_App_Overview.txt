================================================================================
ROES - ROYAL ORGANIZING ECOSYSTEM
Application Overview: What It Is, Who Uses It, and How It Works
================================================================================

Document type : Product and system overview (text only)
Audience      : Company / management / technical and non-technical stakeholders
Product state : Front end complete (website + mobile app) running on sample
                data. Back end is the next phase.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT IS ROES?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ROES (Royal Organizing Ecosystem) is a city-management platform that connects
the three levels of a city's community in one organized system:

    MAYOR  ->  ADMIN  ->  USER (RESIDENT)

It gives the mayor's office one place to see and manage the whole city, gives
the managers of each building or public place (admins) the tools to look after
their own community, and gives residents a simple mobile app to stay informed,
raise problems, and take part in community life.

The platform has two surfaces:

  1. A WEBSITE for the mayor (desktop-first dashboard).
  2. A MOBILE APP (Android / iOS) shared by admins and residents. The same
     sign-in is used by both; the user's role decides which home screen opens.

The sample data used in this version is set in Kuala Lumpur (16 places,
about 1,100 residents).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE PROBLEM IT SOLVES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Cities and large residential communities usually run on scattered channels:
group chats, notice boards, phone calls and paper forms. As a result:

  - Announcements do not reliably reach the right people.
  - Complaints get lost or have no visible status.
  - The mayor's office has no live picture of how the city is growing or what
    residents are raising.
  - Neighbour disputes escalate because there is no neutral place to settle them.
  - Safety information and donation drives have no single trusted home.

ROES replaces these with one structured, role-based system where every message,
complaint and rule has a clear sender, a clear audience and a visible status.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. ROLES AND HIERARCHY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

There are three roles. Authority flows downward, and each role only sees and
manages what belongs to it.

There are also two kinds of PLACES in the city:

  - RESIDENTIAL places: buildings or communities where people live (for example
    condominiums, apartments, landed communities).
  - NON-RESIDENTIAL places: public or commercial places (for example a station,
    a mall, a park). These have no registered residents.

Summary table:

  ROLE                     SURFACE        SCOPE
  -----------------------  -------------  --------------------------------------
  Mayor                    Website        Whole city: admins, users, places,
                                          announcements, safety policies,
                                          donations, public complaints, analytics
  Admin (residential)      Mobile app     Own residence: private announcements,
                                          house rules (residents and visitors),
                                          private complaints, analytics
  Admin (non-residential)  Mobile app     Own place: public announcements, one
                                          set of house rules, complaints about
                                          that place
  User (resident)          Mobile app     Own account; public and own-residence
                                          announcements; house rules; reports;
                                          complaints; search; safety info;
                                          donations; game

3.1 MAYOR
  - Sees the full picture of the city through a live dashboard.
  - Creates admins, or gives the admin role to an existing user.
  - Assigns an admin to a place and can change that assignment later.
  - Can freeze (suspend) or reactivate an admin.
  - Can view all users and all places, but personal details are READ-ONLY.
    The mayor never edits a person's personal information.
  - Publishes public announcements.
  - Creates and publishes safety policies that appear in every resident's app.
  - Creates and manages donation campaigns.
  - Receives public complaints sent directly to the mayor and replies to them.

3.2 ADMIN
  An admin manages one place, and what the admin can do depends on the place type.

  Residential admin:
    - Sees the number of residents and a resident growth chart.
    - Posts PRIVATE notices that only the residents of that residence can see.
    - Maintains TWO sets of house rules: one for residents, one for visitors.
    - Receives and answers private complaints from residents.
    - Has a searchable list of residents with read-only details.

  Non-residential (public place) admin:
    - Posts PUBLIC notices.
    - Maintains ONE set of house rules (for visitors).
    - Receives and answers complaints about that place.
    - Has no registered residents.

  Both kinds of admin can:
    - Search any place on the city map and read its visitor rules.
    - Edit their own email and phone number and profile photo.
    - Read mayor announcements and notices about their own place.

3.3 USER (RESIDENT)
  - Registers in the app and verifies their email, choosing their residence.
  - Manages their own account (name, phone, avatar style, password).
  - Reads public notices and the private notices of their own residence.
  - Reads house rules (resident rules and visitor rules).
  - Sends complaints, starts neighbour reports, and reports places.
  - Searches places on the map by name and by location.
  - Reads safety information and can call emergency services in one tap.
  - Views donation campaigns and plays the community recycling game.

4. THE MAYOR WEBSITE (FEATURES AND SCREENS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Sign-in
  Mayor-only login with email and password, a "forgot password" flow, and a
  "keep me signed in" option.

Dashboard
  Key figures at a glance: total users, new users this month, active admins,
  open complaints. Also: a resident growth chart (30 days or 12 months), a live
  activity feed, users per residence, complaints summary, donation campaigns,
  and the admin roster. A "Live" indicator simulates real-time events (new
  resident, new complaint, new donation) and can be paused.

New Users (growth analysis)
  See who joined during: this month, the last 3 months, a specific month, this
  year, or a specific year. Shows a chart, a breakdown by residence, a table,
  and CSV export.

Admins
  Create an admin, give the admin role to an existing user, change an admin's
  place, freeze or reactivate an admin. Personal details are read-only.

Users
  Every user in the city with search and filters. Personal details are
  read-only.

Places
  Create, edit, remove and assign admins to places. Two views: "Map and list"
  (an interactive city map with pins - houses for residential, buildings for
  non-residential) and "Manage places" (with an "Unassigned" filter that shows
  places still waiting for an admin). Each place's details include its resident
  and visitor rules.

Announcements
  Public and private announcements. The mayor can publish public ones.

Safety Policies
  Create, edit, publish, unpublish and delete safety policies (for example fire
  safety, flood preparedness, emergency numbers). Published policies appear in
  each resident's profile in the mobile app.

Donations
  Create and manage donation campaigns with a title, description, goal amount,
  cover image, category and deadline. Shows total raised, active campaigns,
  number of donors and combined goal. Campaigns can be closed and reopened.

Public Complaints
  An inbox of complaints that residents addressed to the mayor. The mayor can
  reply (quick-reply suggestions are available) and update the status.

Profile
  Mayor details, security and appearance (Light or Dark theme).

5. THE MOBILE APP: SHARED FEATURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Animated splash screen on launch.
  - One sign-in for admins and residents; after sign-in a personal "Hello,
    {name} - welcome back" screen with the user's photo appears, then the role's
    home screen opens.
  - Resident registration in 4 steps with email-code verification and the
    password entered twice. New residents see a one-time "Welcome to ROES".
  - Forgot-password flow with a verification code.
  - EXPLORE MAP: a pannable, zoomable map of the city with search by name. Tap
    any place to see its details and the rules a visitor must follow there.
  - Profile with photo upload.
  - Appearance: Light or Dark theme. Dark mode uses a "Luxury Black and Gold"
    look; light mode uses the royal blue and gold brand colours.
  - Haptic feedback and notification toggles.
  - Sign out (with confirmation) from the profile and from the avatar menu on the
    home screen.

6. THE MOBILE APP: ADMIN SIDE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Home / Dashboard
  Residential admin: number of residents, complaints, rules, notices and a
  growth chart. Public-place admin: complaints, rules and notices.

Notices
  Residential admin: posts PRIVATE notices (only that residence's residents see
  them). Public-place admin: posts PUBLIC notices.

House Rules
  Residential admin: two sets - rules for residents and rules for visitors.
  Public-place admin: one set for visitors. Rules can be added, edited,
  reordered and removed. Residents can flag "Someone is breaking this rule".

Inbox (Complaints)
  Complaints are shown as a chat with a status timeline (for example New,
  In review, Done), plus quick replies for common answers. Place reports and
  neighbour-report escalations appear here as well.

Residents (residential admins only)
  Searchable resident list with read-only details.

Notifications
  A bell icon leads to a screen showing mayor announcements and notices about
  the admin's own place.

Profile
  Admins can edit their own email and phone number and profile photo, and change
  the theme.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. THE MOBILE APP: RESIDENT SIDE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The resident app has five tabs: Home, Community, Explore, Help, Me.

HOME
  Greeting, residence card, quick actions, a "following up" list of open
  complaints and reports, and the latest notices.

COMMUNITY
  - Notices: public notices plus the private notices of the resident's own
    residence; filter by source (mayor / residence / public place); unseen
    notices are marked with a dot.
  - House Rules: switch between rules for residents and rules for visitors, with
    a "Someone is breaking this rule" action.
  - Reports: place reports posted about the resident's own residence.

EXPLORE
  The city map with search. Tap a place to read its visitor rules and any
  reports made about it.

HELP
  Complaints - a resident chooses where the complaint goes:
    (a) to their own residence's admin (private),
    (b) to the admin of a specific place, or
    (c) to the mayor (public complaint).
  Each complaint is a chat with a status timeline so the resident can always see
  where it stands.

  Neighbour Reports - when a resident has a problem with a neighbour, a GROUP
  CHAT is opened between the resident, the neighbour and the "ROES Mediator"
  (an AI helper). The mediator:
    - holds back rude, threatening or shouting messages and shows a warning,
    - after three warnings, sends the conversation to the residence admin,
    - lets the people involved mark the issue as resolved or escalate it to
      the admin themselves.

  Place Reports - "Report a place" posts a notice-style report about any place
  (the resident's own residence or a public place). Residents of that residence
  can see it in Community > Reports, anyone can see it in the place's sheet on
  the Explore map, and that place's admin sees it from an Inbox banner.

ME
  - Profile and appearance (Light / Dark).
  - Manage Account: name, phone, avatar style, change password, delete account.
    Email and residence are locked.
  - Safety and Policies: a one-tap emergency call (999) and the community safety
    policies published by the mayor.
  - Donations: active campaigns with progress bars; pledging is a demo (no real
    payment yet).
  - Tidy Town game (see next section).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. TIDY TOWN: THE COMMUNITY RECYCLING GAME
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Purpose: encourage better recycling habits and daily engagement in a light,
friendly way.

How it plays:
  - 45 seconds, 3 lives. Everyday items fall one at a time (for example a glass
    bottle, newspaper, cardboard box, milk carton, drink can, banana peel,
    greasy pizza box, sweet wrapper, cigarette butt, chicken bone).
  - The player swipes the item left into the green RECYCLE bin or right into the
    grey RUBBISH bin (or taps the bin).
  - A correct sort scores points; streaks raise a score multiplier (up to x3).
  - A wrong sort or a missed item costs a life and shows a short educational tip
    explaining why the item belongs where it does.
  - Items use their own natural colours, so colour never gives the answer away.

Leaderboard:
  - Each player chooses a public NICKNAME; real names are never shown.
  - Rankings are shown as a podium and ranked list, with two views: whole city
    and "My residence".
  - After a game the player sees their rank and best score.

Presentation:
  - 3D-style item and bin artwork, bins whose lids open as the player drags an
    item toward them, and an animated town in the background (skyline, people and
    pets walking, cars, a bus, a recycling truck, birds and clouds) that is
    daytime in light mode and an evening scene in dark mode.

9. HOW THE PIECES WORK TOGETHER (TYPICAL FLOWS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

FLOW A - Onboarding a new resident
  1. The resident downloads the app and registers, choosing their residence.
  2. They verify their email with a code.
  3. They appear in the mayor's "New Users" analysis and in their admin's
     resident list.

FLOW B - Setting up a place
  1. The mayor creates a place on the website (residential or non-residential).
  2. The mayor creates an admin, or promotes an existing user, and assigns the
     admin to the place.
  3. The admin signs in on the app and sets up notices and house rules.

FLOW C - A resident reports a problem
  1. The resident opens Help and chooses where to send the complaint: own admin,
     a specific place's admin, or the mayor.
  2. The recipient replies in the chat and updates the status.
  3. The resident follows progress on their Home screen under "following up".

FLOW D - A neighbour dispute
  1. The resident starts a neighbour report; a group chat with the neighbour and
     the AI mediator opens.
  2. The mediator filters aggressive messages and warns the sender.
  3. The issue is marked resolved, or it escalates to the residence admin after
     three warnings (or on request).

FLOW E - City-wide communication
  1. The mayor publishes a public announcement, a safety policy or a donation
     campaign on the website.
  2. These appear in the residents' and admins' apps.

FLOW F - Place information
  Anyone can search a place on the Explore map and read the rules for visiting
  it, so visitors know what is expected before they arrive.

10. PRIVACY AND CONTROL PRINCIPLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Role-based access: each person only sees what their role allows.
  - The mayor can see users and residences but cannot edit anyone's personal
    details; admins see only their own residents.
  - Private notices and private complaints stay inside the residence.
  - The game shows only nicknames, never real names.
  - Aggressive or abusive messages in neighbour reports are held back
    automatically.
  - Residents can edit their own account and delete it.
  - Admins can be frozen by the mayor without deleting their records.

11. TECHNOLOGY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Mayor website (folder fe)
  Next.js (App Router), TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, Radix-based UI components,
  Recharts (charts), Leaflet (map), Zustand (state), React Hook Form with Zod
  (forms and validation). Light and Dark themes.

Mobile app (folder apk)
  Expo and React Native with TypeScript, Expo Router (navigation), Reanimated
  (animation), react-native-svg (game artwork), Zustand (state, saved on the
  device). One codebase builds for Android and iOS and can also run as a web
  preview. An installable Android APK can be produced with EAS Build.

Back end (folder be)
  Not started. It begins after the front end and the product idea are finished.

Project structure:
    ROSE/
      fe/   mayor website
      apk/  mobile app (admins and residents)
      be/   back end (planned)

IMPORTANT NOTE ON THE DEMO
  This version has NO back end. All data lives in the browser (website) or on
  the device (mobile app) and is generated sample data. Specifically:
    - Sign-in, email codes and password reset are simulated; codes are shown on
      screen instead of being emailed.
    - The AI mediator and neighbour replies run as simple on-device rules.
    - Donation pledges do not move any money.
    - The map is a static preview image with pins placed from each place's
      coordinates.
    - Passwords are stored in plain text for the demo only.
  Because of this, the demo shows exactly how the product looks and behaves, but
  it is not yet connected to a live system.

13. NEXT PHASE: BACK END AND GO-LIVE ROADMAP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Real authentication: hashed passwords, real email and SMS verification,
     secure sessions, role enforcement on the server.
  2. A database and an API behind every action the screens already use, so the
     website and the app share one live set of data (for example a safety policy
     or donation post the mayor creates appears in the residents' apps).
  3. A real AI moderation / mediation service for neighbour reports.
  4. Push notifications for notices, complaint replies and report updates.
  5. A live map service in place of the static preview image.
  6. A payment provider for donations.
  7. File storage for photos and cover images instead of embedding them.
  8. Security review, testing, and production deployment (website hosting, app
     store release).

The front end has been built so that these additions only replace the data layer
behind the screens; the screens themselves are not expected to change.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
14. KEY BENEFITS (SUMMARY FOR DECISION MAKERS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - One system for the whole city: mayor, building managers and residents.
  - Faster, more reliable communication: notices reach exactly the right
    audience.
  - Accountability: every complaint has an owner, a chat and a status.
  - Data for decisions: live resident growth, complaint volume and donation
    progress for the mayor's office.
  - Safer communities: central safety policies and a one-tap emergency call.
  - Calmer neighbourhoods: AI-assisted mediation before issues reach an admin.
  - Community engagement: donations and a recycling game that rewards good
    habits.
  - Modern, polished experience: Light and Dark themes, smooth animation,
    consistent design on web and mobile.
